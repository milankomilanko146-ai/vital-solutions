I Told My 8-Year-Old “Maybe Next Time” for Two Years. Then I Saw What My Friend Was Doing Differently.

I tried pills, gels and supplements. Then a friend showed me the one thing I was missing, and I wish I had seen it two years earlier.

Written by Adriana B. |

I lied to nine people in the park last Sunday.

I told them I just wanted to “enjoy the sun.”

The truth? After about ten minutes of walking, my knees ached so deeply that I had to sit down on the first bench I saw. A deep, heavy ache, as if someone had filled the joints with hot sand.

My friend Mihaela kept walking with the others, laughing and talking.

That surprised me. A year earlier, she was the one who always sat down first. She used to rub her knees and say, “I think this is just how it is now.”

Now she was walking ahead of everyone.

Then my son Matei, who is 8, came running back with the ball and asked me to kick it with him.

I said, “Mami is tired today. Maybe next time.”

He just nodded and ran back to the others.

That was the worst part. Not the ache. The nod.

He had stopped asking me why.

That evening, one question kept going round in my head: what did Mihaela know that I didn’t?

The answer was so simple I almost laughed. But to understand it, you first need to see how I got there.

It Never Starts With a Big Moment

At first it was just a little stiffness in the morning. I blamed the weather. A bad night’s sleep. Too much walking the day before.

Then it started to hurt on the stairs. Then when I got up from a chair. Then when I got out of the car.

I live on the third floor, and without wanting to, I started counting the steps.

Little by little, I started avoiding things. I stopped kneeling down to play with Matei. I said no to long walks. I took the car even for short distances.

“Next time” became my answer to everything.

When my mother called and asked how I was, I said, “Fine, just a bit tired.”

I wasn’t tired. I was scared.

Because one thought kept coming back at night, the one I never said out loud: if my knees feel like this now, what will they feel like in ten years?

Everything I Tried (and Why Nothing Lasted)

I did what most people do.

I started with pills from the pharmacy. They helped for a few hours, but I didn’t want to take them every day. I always felt I was only hiding the problem.

Then I bought gels, at least four different ones. Each one felt good for about an hour. Then the ache came back as if nothing had happened.

Then supplements, because everyone says you have to work on it “from the inside.” I took them for months and noticed nothing.

After that came a knee support, special insoles and hot water bottles.

By then I had started to believe what people kept telling me: “It’s your age. It’s normal. You’ll get used to it.”

I almost did.

Then I saw Mihaela in that park.

“It’s Not Your Age. You’re Only Using Half of the Answer.”

The next day I called her.

“Mihaela, what are you doing for your knees? Tell me everything.”

She laughed. “I was exactly where you are. Then I noticed something almost embarrassingly simple about every cream I had ever bought.”

“What?”

“They only ever do half of the job.”

I didn’t understand. Not yet.

So she explained it to me, the way she had worked it out herself.

The Half-Problem Nobody Mentions

“Think about how your knee feels,” she said. “Some days it’s hot and achy, and all you want is something cool and fresh. Other days it’s tight and stiff, like a rusty hinge, and all you want is warmth.”

She was right. Both were me.

“Now think about what’s in your cabinet.”

A cooling gel gives you the fresh feeling. For a little while. And no warmth at all.

A warming gel gives you warmth. And not a trace of freshness.

Every cream I had ever bought picked ONE side.

I had been choosing. Switching. Stacking one on top of the other. And I had never once had both sides at the same time, on the same spot.

“And doing it by hand,” she added, “a cold compress, then a warm towel, takes a bowl of ice, a heating pad and a sofa. That’s not a life that happens in a park.”

That was the half of the problem I had never noticed.

Then she opened her bag.

The Small Tube in Her Bag

She took out a small tube, white and orange with a dark blue top.

LioStrong. A 2-in-1 balm.

My first thought was, “Another cream.” I said it out loud, too.

She wasn’t offended. She just pointed at the tube.

“Read it. Double enhancing effect. I thought exactly what you’re thinking. But this one has two sides.”

Cool on One Side. Warm on the Other.

Here is what she showed me, and what I later checked on the label myself.

The cool side. Menthol, camphor and eucalyptus oil give a fresh, cool feeling the moment you put it on.

The warm side. Capsicum, an extract of hot pepper, brings a gentle, spreading warmth a few minutes later.

So you get fresh and warm together, on the same spot, from one tube.

The balm also contains plants Romanians have known for a long time:

  • Comfrey (tătăneasă), a classic plant extract in massage balms
  • Horse chestnut seed extract
  • Siberian fir, maritime pine and lemon oils, which give it a fresh, forest-like smell

And because you massage it in, the area around the knee also gets something it may not have had in a long time: slow, gentle attention.

That was the piece I had been missing. Gels did one thing. This was made to do both.

I Needed Proof, So I Asked Around

I’m a person who needs proof. So I started asking.

Mihaela had been using it for about two months. The first thing she noticed wasn’t a big change. It was that her knees felt less “locked” in the morning.

Then I noticed something in my own building.

Doamna Elena lives below me. For years I had heard her stop on every landing, one breath at a time. The week I started asking questions, I heard her go up the stairs in one go. The next day I met her at the mailboxes, and a small white tube was sticking out of her coat pocket. The same tube. She whispered, “Don’t tell my daughter. She thinks I’m too stubborn to try anything new.”

Then my mother. At 71 she tells me “it’s age” every single time. When I mentioned the tube on the phone, she went quiet. Then she said, “Write me the name. I’ll ask your brother to order it.”

My mother never asks for anything.

Mihaela. Doamna Elena. My own mother, asking for a name. Three different lives, one small tube.

I stopped thinking I was alone. I was just the last one to find out.

Messages From Readers

The First Two Weeks

I ordered it that night. I’ll tell you why I stopped hesitating in a minute, because first I want you to see how it went.

On the first evening I put a little on each knee and rubbed it in quickly, the way you rub in hand cream. I felt the coolness first, then a gentle warmth. The smell was strong, like herbs and eucalyptus, and it took me a day or two to get used to it.

On the fourth day I thought, “Maybe it’s the same as the rest.”

When I told Mihaela, she shook her head.

“You’re not massaging it, you’re just spreading it. Take two minutes. Slow circles around the whole knee, until it’s absorbed. The massage is half of it.”

So I did it properly, every morning and every evening.

By the end of the first week, something small happened, and I almost missed it.

I stood up from the kitchen chair, and I didn’t hold on to the table.

“Mami, You Didn’t Say You Were Tired Today”

It was around the third week. I came home, and Matei was standing in the hallway with the ball under his arm. He didn’t even ask. He just looked at me.

I put my bag down and said, “Give me five minutes, and we’ll go to the park.”

He didn’t believe me at first.

We kicked the ball for half an hour. I walked, I turned, I even ran a little. And that heavy, burning ache I used to have wasn’t there.

On the way back, Matei took my hand and said:

“Mami, you didn’t say you were tired today.”

I had to look away so he wouldn’t see my face.

“119 Lei for a Tube? Really?”

I know that’s what you’re thinking, because I thought it too.

The price is 119 lei. Yes, it costs more than a simple gel from the pharmacy. When you have a family and bills to pay, 119 lei is not a small amount.

So I looked at it differently.

A cheap gel works on one side only. That is exactly why I had bought so many of them. LioStrong has a double enhancing effect: a cool side and a warm side. I wasn’t paying for a tube. I was paying for the other half I had never had.

And I thought about everything I had already bought: pills, gels, supplements, insoles, the knee support. I had never added it up, but I knew it was far more than 119 lei. Not one of them had gotten me off that bench.

So the question wasn’t “is it expensive?” It was “what is it worth to finally try both sides?” For me, it was worth it.

And there was one more thing that helped me decide. I didn’t have to pay anything in advance.

Two Choices, One Bus Stop

When I think about it now, there were only ever two choices.

Choice one: keep doing what I had been doing. Another pill, another gel, another “maybe next time,” another year of watching everyone else walk ahead.

Choice two: try something different for 119 lei, pay only when it arrives, and finally find out.

I waited almost two years to make the second choice. I’m still a little angry at myself for that.

Last month I was waiting at the bus stop, and a woman next to me was holding on to the pole with one hand and rubbing her knee with the other.

I recognized that look.

I smiled at her and said, “Don’t laugh. Let me tell you about a small white tube.”

If my story sounds familiar, you don’t have to wait as long as I did.
