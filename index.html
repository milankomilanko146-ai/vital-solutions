The Small Mistake I Didn’t Notice That Kept My Knees Hurting for Two Years

I tried pills, gels and supplements, and the pain always came back. A friend’s physiotherapist sister explained what most people miss, and I wish I had heard it two years earlier.

Written by Adriana B. |

It was a sunny Sunday, and we had gone to the park with friends and our kids.

After about ten minutes of walking, I had to sit down on a bench.

My knees ached with a deep, heavy ache, as if someone had filled the joints with hot sand. I told everyone I just wanted to “enjoy the sun.”

My friend Mihaela kept walking with the others, laughing and talking.

That surprised me. A year earlier, she was the one who always sat down first. She used to rub her knees and say, “I think this is just how it is now.”

Now she was walking as if the pain had never existed.

My son Matei, who is 8, came running back and asked me to kick the ball with him.

I said, “Mami is tired today. Maybe next time.”

He just nodded and ran back to the others.

That was the worst part.

That evening I could only think of one question: what changed for Mihaela?

The answer was simpler than I expected. But first, let me tell you how my knees got to this point.

It Starts Small, and Then It Takes Over

At first, it was just a little stiffness in the morning. I told myself it was the weather. Or a bad night’s sleep. Or that I had walked too much the day before.

Then it started to hurt on the stairs. Then when I got up from a chair. Then when I got out of the car.

I live on the third floor, and without wanting to, I started counting the steps.

Little by little, I started avoiding things. I stopped kneeling down to play with Matei. I said no to long walks. I took the car even for short distances.

“Next time” became my answer to everything.

When my mother called and asked how I was, I said, “Fine, just a bit tired.”

I wasn’t tired. I was worried.

Because one thought kept coming back at night, one I never said out loud: if my knees feel like this now, what will they feel like in ten years?

What I Tried, and Why It Never Lasted

I did what most people do.

I started with pills from the pharmacy. They helped for a few hours, but I didn’t want to take them every day. I always felt I was only hiding the problem.

Then I bought warming gels, at least four different ones. Each one felt good for about an hour. Then the pain came back as if nothing had happened.

Then I tried supplements, because everyone says you have to work on it “from the inside.” I took them for months and noticed nothing.

After that came a knee support, special insoles and hot water bottles.

By then, I had started to believe what people kept telling me: “It’s your age. It’s normal. You’ll get used to it.”

I almost did.

Then I saw Mihaela in that park.

“It’s Not Your Age. You’re Only Treating Half of It.”

The next day I called her.

“Mihaela, what are you doing for your knees? Tell me everything.”

She laughed. Then she said, “I was exactly where you are. And my sister changed the way I think about it.”

Her sister is a physiotherapist. When Mihaela complained about her knees, her sister asked her one simple question:

“When you start walking after sitting for a while, does your knee feel tight and stiff, like it needs to warm up first?”

Mihaela said yes. Every time.

“Then you’re stuck in the stiffness trap,” her sister told her. “Most people stay in it for years, because they only treat half of the problem.”

Half of the problem.

That was the small mistake I hadn’t noticed. All those years I had only tried to quiet the pain. I had never done anything about the stiffness around the knee.

The Stiffness Trap

This is how Mihaela explained it to me, and for the first time it made sense.

When a knee hurts, the body tries to protect it. The muscles around the joint tighten, a bit like a fist closing around it. You don’t decide this. It happens on its own.

But a tight knee hurts more when you move it. So you start moving it less, or more carefully and stiffly. And the less naturally it moves, the stiffer it gets.

Pain makes you stiff. Stiffness makes more pain. And it goes round and round.

That is why a knee can feel fine at noon and like a rusty hinge in the evening, or the next morning.

Now look at what most of us tried.

Pills travel through the whole body. They can quiet the pain for a few hours, but they are not aimed at the tight area around the knee.

Simple warming gels warm the skin for a short time. That feels nice, but it covers only one side of the problem.

To get out of the trap, you need to do two things together:

Calm the ache, and loosen the stiffness, right where it is.

That is exactly what Mihaela had started doing.
